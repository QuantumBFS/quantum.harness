Challenge #113: Tracking the Right d²−1 Control Directions

https://github.com/QuantumBFS/quantum.harness/issues/113

Device-side cross-curvature updates keep the optimizer at rank 15 yet recover 5/5 strong-mismatch trials, outperforming fixed rank 15, permanent rank 30, and raw rank 40 searches.

ChallengeApproachResultsHighlight

Challenge

Why the model Hessian must transfer to a noisy device without losing its scaling advantage.

A differentiable simulator can optimize a quantum gate and expose its local Hessian, but a real device may differ through drift and only return noisy scalar fidelity measurements. For a two-qubit gate, the physically relevant phase-free unitary tangent space has dimension d2-1=15, even though the pulse has 40 Fourier coefficients. The challenge is to use those 15 directions on the mismatched device without assuming that the simulator's exact eigenvectors remain correct.

Significance

Device evaluations are the expensive resource. Searching all 40 pulse coordinates discards known quantum geometry, while permanently widening from 15 to 30 only postpones the same scaling problem. A rank-preserving update would retain the d2-1 scaling law while adapting to model error.

Approach

A model-informed, device-corrected subspace with a hard rank constraint.

Strict query-only closed loop
Model basis
Leading 15 eigenvectors of the calibrated simulator Hessian

Conservative prior

The model supplies a well-conditioned rank-15 coordinate system. When the device does not confirm the target, the algorithm probes only how that active space couples to two temporary orthogonal scouts. It estimates the 15×2 cross block with four-point mixed differences, updates the local eigenspace, and immediately compresses back to rank 15. The scouts are measurements, not new deployed optimization parameters.

Highlight

What this changes about the interpretation of d²−1.

What's innovative

The method treats d2-1 as a hard rank constraint rather than a frozen list of simulator eigenvectors. Temporary scouts estimate only the missing cross-curvature information, so model structure is corrected instead of discarded.

Significance of the output

The earlier top-k sweep showed where fixed bases fail; the tracked experiment explains how to respond without blind widening. At the hardest tested mismatch, fixed rank 15 changed from 2/5 to 5/5 success and surpassed permanent rank 30 despite using half as many deployed coordinates.

Broader impact

For larger gates, raw pulse dimension may grow far faster than the local unitary tangent space. Model-informed rank constraints plus query-only rotation offer a path toward calibration whose optimization dimension follows system geometry, while still tolerating moderate simulator-to-device drift.

Limits and next test
  • The device is simulated with binomial fidelity noise, not real hardware.
  • The headline study uses one Hamiltonian seed and one drift direction; five seeds cover optimizer and measurement randomness only.
  • Each update costs O(kr) queries for active rank k and r scouts; structured scouts or simultaneous mixed-derivative estimators may reduce the constant.
  • The next scaling test should repeat the invariant and tracking protocol for larger d while holding the deployed rank at d²−1.
