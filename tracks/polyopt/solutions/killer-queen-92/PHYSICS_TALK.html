The physical target

A bulk particle/hole gap in an infinite truncated Bose–Hubbard model

H = −t Σ⟨ij⟩(bi†bj + bj†bi) + (U/2) Σi ni(ni−1) − μ Σi ni,   U = 1

Physics

  • Small t: repulsion favors roughly one boson per site—a unit-filling Mott state.
  • Larger t: hopping favors delocalization, number fluctuations, and phase coherence.
  • The bulk gap measures the minimum energy cost of a localized bulk excitation above the infinite-volume ground state.

We monitor density ρ0 = ⟨n0⟩, unit-filling deviation F0 = ⟨(n0−1)2⟩, and hopping coherence K0.

Thermodynamic interpretation of ED

The ED numbers provide no upper or lower bound on the bulk gap

What ED legitimately provides

  • a sign and normalization check for the hopping Hamiltonian;
  • a check of the occupation cutoff and number-sector code;
  • exact finite-cluster density, fluctuation, and coherence;
  • a qualitative energy scale against which to judge whether an SDP upper bound is loose.

What it does not provide

  • no infinite-volume ground state;
  • no finite-size scaling—we used only radius one;
  • no rigorous upper or lower bulk-gap bound;
  • no controlled removal of hyperbolic boundary effects.

Why hyperbolic open boundaries are especially dangerous

The number of sites grows exponentially with radius, so a non-vanishing fraction remains near the boundary. An open-ball spectrum can therefore be dominated by boundary coordination and boundary modes even at large radius.

Our generated {12,4} balls contain 5, 17, and 53 sites at radii 1, 2, and 3; the newest shell contains 4, 12, and 36 sites respectively. The boundary is not becoming negligible in this sequence.

The finite ED values are useful physics diagnostics, but it would be wrong to present 0.305, 0.276, or 0.205 as estimates with a known relation to Δbulk.
SDP without assuming SDP expertise

Replace the infinite wavefunction by a consistency table of local correlations

Instead of truncating the number of lattice sites, the method forgets the global wavefunction and retains expectation values such as ⟨A⟩ and ⟨A†B⟩ for selected local operators. These numbers must obey exact algebraic and positivity relations if they come from any quantum state.

Why a positive-semidefinite matrix appears

MAB = ω(A†B)   ⪰   0 because c†Mc = ω[(ΣcAA)†(ΣcBB)] ≥ 0

An SDP is simply a convex search over such correlation tables subject to linear equalities and positive-semidefinite matrix constraints.

The assumed-gap condition

½ω(A†[H,A] − [H,A†]A) ≥ γ[ω(A†A) − |ω(A)|2]

The left side is the excitation-energy quadratic form; the right side is γ times the variance. If the bulk gap is at least γ, this must hold for every local excitation operator A.

The term |ω(A)|2 is nonlinear in the state. State-polynomial lifting introduces extra moment variables for products of expectation values, restoring a linear matrix problem at fixed γ.

Conceptually, the SDP asks: “Could these local numbers belong to any infinite ground state whose local excitations all cost at least γ?”
How a local calculation reaches the infinite lattice

Locality removes the far-away Hamiltonian exactly—not approximately

Root-supported probe

Let A act only at the orange root. Split the infinite Hamiltonian as

H = Htouching root + Hfar away,   [Hfar away, A] = 0.

Therefore [H,A] is computed exactly from the root on-site term and the z hopping terms incident to the root. We have not imposed an open boundary; far-away terms cancel from this particular commutator because they commute with A.

Every genuine infinite ground state supplies a local correlation table satisfying these constraints. If no such table exists, no such infinite state exists.

This is the thermodynamic mechanism: retain the infinite-state quantifier, use locality to evaluate local commutators, and exclude impossible local restrictions. No finite-size extrapolation is involved.

Root-local outer test

An exact thermodynamic implication built from an incomplete set of local probes

What is represented

  • A pseudo-table of expectations on the root and its nearest neighbors—not a wavefunction on that window.
  • The exact cutoff algebra Mnmax+1(ℂ) using matrix units Ers.
  • All one-site root probes A = ΣrscrsErs(0).
  • Only U(1)-invariant states; charged one-point functions are set to zero.

What is imposed

  1. local positivity of the correlation table;
  2. normalization and exact matrix-unit identities;
  3. stationarity, ω([H,A]) = 0;
  4. positive covariance;
  5. the assumed-gap matrix at fixed γ.

Exact aspect

If an infinite U(1)-invariant ground state has gap at least γ, its local moments must satisfy every constraint in this rooted model. Thus exact infeasibility would exclude γ directly in the thermodynamic limit.

Approximate/weak aspect

We test only one-site root excitations and only a subset of the full state-polynomial hierarchy. A feasible pseudo-table need not extend to a real infinite state. Feasibility therefore proves nothing about the existence of a gap.

“Thermodynamic” does not mean “already accurate.” It describes the logical target of an infeasibility result, while the relaxation level controls how strong that result is.
How to read an SDP run

The certificate direction is the reverse of a usual variational gap estimate

✓
Feasible at γ. The relaxation found locally consistent pseudo-correlations. The gap may be above γ, below γ, or zero. We simply failed to rule γ out.
×
Infeasible at γ. No local correlation table satisfies even these necessary conditions. In exact arithmetic, an infinite U(1)-invariant ground state cannot have gap ≥ γ, so ΔU(1) < γ.
?
Solver error or inaccurate PSD violation. No scientific conclusion.
ΓL,d = largest γ not yet excluded,   ΓL,d ↓ Δbulk as the full nested hierarchy grows.

Our current rooted model is a valid necessary outer test but is not yet the complete nested (L,d) hierarchy. We should therefore call its endpoints first-level floating candidates, not a convergent sequence already demonstrated in code.

This method produces upper bounds on the gap. It is not the ordinary ground-energy SDP that gives lower bounds on E0.

What geometry the first SDP can see

At root-only level, {12,4} and the line graph are mathematically identical

For a root-supported A, neighbor-neighbor hopping terms commute with A. The gap commutator therefore sees only:

  • the on-site root Hamiltonian;
  • the z bonds incident to the root;
  • the occupation cutoff and model parameters.

Both {12,4} and L({8,3}) have z = 4, so their root-local outer tests are identical. The two triangles visible to finite ED do not enter a one-site commutator.

Professor’s conclusion

What has been accomplished, and what physics remains

Established

  • Exact finite-cutoff bosonic matrix algebra, including the corrected truncated commutator.
  • Validated construction of the three hyperbolic local geometries.
  • An atomic state-polynomial SDP reproducing the exact thermodynamic gap.
  • A complete matrix-degree Definition 2.4 assembler with exact U(1) charge blocks, plus a separately labelled TS2 sparse family.
  • An exact ladder-degree cross-check basis obtained by charge-adapted graded row reduction, sharing the finite-matrix multiplication engine.
  • Clarabel and Mosek solve paths, six local-observable objectives, resumable bisection, and an independent algebraic dual checker.
  • An independent finite-cluster ED baseline and reproducible datasets.

Not yet established

  • No accepted production ΓL,d endpoint and therefore no demonstrated numerical convergence yet.
  • No geometry-sensitive thermodynamic comparison between {12,4} and L({8,3}).
  • No reliable nmax=2 or 3 non-atomic upper-bound endpoint.
  • No unrestricted-state result beyond the U(1)-invariant sector.
  • No production infeasibility ray has yet passed the exact projection, 256-bit Arb, PSD, and positive-margin checks.

Research verdict: the primary matrix hierarchy is now implemented and structurally tested, but the scientific campaign is not complete. A formal COMPLETE level contains every Definition 2.4 monomial; TS2 is a practical sparse principal-submatrix level and will never be plotted as if it were formal completeness. Each excluded γ would mean that no U(1)-invariant KMS ground state can have bulk gap at least γ—not that a smaller gap has been proved.
