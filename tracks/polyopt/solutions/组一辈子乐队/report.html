Coarse-grained ground-state certification for spin chains

A scalable MPS-guided coarse-RDM relaxation reaches a stable D=6, depth-60 numerical lower estimate for the infinite XXZ chain while preserving a clear boundary between numerical evidence and exact certification.

Challenge

The problem and why it matters.

Ground-state lower bounds turn numerical evidence into certification, but ordinary NPA semidefinite relaxations grow exponentially with support size. Challenge #49 asks whether renormalization-group coarse graining and structure exploitation can extend that reach to large quantum spin systems.

Significance

The implementation demonstrates the core scalable mechanism on an infinite spin chain: frozen MPS contractions replace exponentially growing physical RDM blocks with fixed-size coarse blocks. A poor map can loosen the relaxation but does not exclude a physical state, preserving the lower-bound logic.

Primary point
D=6, depth=60

A frozen MPS tensor defines fixed linear coarse-graining maps. The MPS energy is only a variational upper endpoint; MPS moments are never imposed as lower-bound constraints. U(1) charge sectors split coarse RDMs into real symmetric PSD blocks. The branch also implements and tests standalone spin-flip Z2 parity blocks, but combined U(1)⋊Z2 reduction and Z2 dual reconstruction remain explicitly unsupported.

Highlight

Innovation, significance, and the claim boundary.

What's innovative

Real U(1)-blocked coarse PSD matrices reduce the D=6 depth-60 problem to 1,301,413 real variables while using 23.92 GiB peak memory. An earlier complex Hermitian formulation used about 93.5 GiB already at depth 5, changing the feasible resource regime rather than merely tuning allocations.

Significance of output

The result shows that deep coarse-RDM relaxations can be assembled and solved in the thermodynamic limit with stable numerical diagnostics. The implementation separates the variational MPS input from the lower-bound relaxation, which is essential for a defensible future certificate.

Limitations and next step
  • The reported endpoints are floating-point numerical lower estimates, not independent exact certificates.
  • Publication-grade certification requires interval-enclosed or rational channel coefficients, PSD projection, full dual identity reconstruction, and rigorous residual correction.
  • The issue's requested 1D Heisenberg, J1–J2, and 2D system-size/accuracy targets have not yet been completed.
  • Standalone Z2 reduction is implemented and tested, but combined U(1)⋊Z2 symmetry and Z2 dual reconstruction are not claimed.
