Method: MPS-guided coarse-RDM lower bounds

The goal is to bracket the infinite-chain ground-state energy from both sides. A finite-D matrix product state (MPS) supplies a variational upper bound and, separately, its tensors define coarse-graining maps for a semidefinite program (SDP). The SDP optimizes reduced density matrices (RDMs) subject to positivity and consistency constraints, producing the lower estimate compared in the main figure.

1 · Optimize an infinite MPS

VUMPS finds a translation-invariant two-site MPS for the Δ=2 XXZ chain. Its expectation value ⟨H⟩/N is a variational upper bound. The MPS tensors are then frozen; the SDP does not variationally optimize them again.

2 · Build coarse-graining maps

Contracting m consecutive MPS tensors defines Wₘ, which compresses the interior of a long interval into its virtual boundary space. The two physical edge spins remain explicit, giving physical support n=m+2.

3 · Optimize a hierarchy of RDMs

The primal SDP contains local physical RDMs and coarse RDMs ω⁽ⁿ⁾. Partial-trace, overlap, and renormalization-flow equations force neighboring hierarchy levels to describe one compatible translation-invariant state.

minimize energy density   subject to   ρ ⪰ 0,   ω⁽ⁿ⁾ ⪰ 0,   trace/overlap consistency,   and coarse-graining flow

4 · Combine U(1) blocks with a real scalar domain

Total Sᶻ conservation decomposes every RDM and linear map into charge sectors, eliminating all cross-charge entries. Because the Δ=2 Hamiltonian and chosen state can be represented with real tensors, the implementation also uses real symmetric PSD blocks rather than general complex Hermitian blocks. These are complementary gains: symmetry makes the matrices block sparse; the real domain removes imaginary variables inside each surviving block.

5 · Obtain the lower side

Minimizing the local energy over this relaxed, consistency-constrained RDM set gives E_lb. The reported corrected value accounts for measured feasibility residuals. A clean solve should satisfy E_lb ≤ E_exact.

6 · Compare a certified bracket

The MPS and SDP play different roles: E_lb comes from the coarse-RDM relaxation, while E_VUMPS comes from a trial wavefunction. Together they target E_lb ≤ E₀ ≤ E_VUMPS; the D=64 value in this report is only the upper-side VUMPS reference.

What is new here: the coarse-RDM hierarchy is implemented directly in real U(1) charge blocks, and one frozen VUMPS state is shared across several depths. The measured D=6, m=5 path drops from 98.0 GB to 7.7 GB peak RSS (12.8×), while the shared-state design avoids repeated VUMPS optimization. Together these changes make larger bond dimension D and deeper hierarchy n practical.
Comparison with the paper implementation: the reference workflow uses a general complex MPS representation. That representation is mathematically valid but inefficient for this real Hamiltonian and symmetry sector: it carries real and imaginary components through MPS contractions and Hermitian SDP constraints. Our Float64 MPS and real symmetric blocks exploit the available real structure. The 12.8× figure measures the complete transition to the real U(1)-blocked implementation; it should not be attributed to real arithmetic alone.
Numerical boundary: an SDP point is presented as clean only when the solver status and feasibility residuals pass the repository gate. The m=8/10/20/32 values remain candidate lower estimates because MOSEK stopped with SLOW_PROGRESS; the method description does not upgrade those points into rigorous certificates.

Why n=m+2

ω⁽ᵐ⁺²⁾ = (I ⊗ Wₘ ⊗ I) ρ⁽ᵐ⁺²⁾ (I ⊗ Wₘ† ⊗ I)

Direct correspondence

  • Wₘ contracts the m interior sites.
  • The left and right physical boundary sites remain explicit.
  • The physical support is therefore m + 1 + 1 = m+2.
  • The runner writes its depth directly as result-D6-m<depth>.tsv; the SDP metadata records the physical-support offset as 2.

Accordingly, m={3,6,8,10,20,32} maps to n={5,8,10,12,22,34}. This is the sole mapping used in the comparison below.
