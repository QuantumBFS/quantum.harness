Challenge

The problem and why it matters.

Finite-temperature quantum impurity solvers must represent an interacting impurity coupled to a continuous fermionic bath while controlling errors from bath discretization, tensor-network truncation, and imaginary-time propagation. ImpuriTree tests whether deterministic purification, implicit evolution on a logarithmic time grid, and residual-driven bond expansion can form a trustworthy solver at β=16 and ultimately approach β=100.

Significance

Reliable low-temperature impurity solvers are central to studying strongly correlated quantum materials. A solver that exposes its residuals and separates bath, bond, and time-stepping errors would make tensor-network results easier to trust, compare with CT-QMC, and extend toward demanding multiorbital or electron–phonon problems.

Approach

How the challenge was solved.

Controlled approximation

The interacting thermal state is represented by a purified tensor network. The calculation approximates the continuous bath with nine poles per spin and controls tensor-network and propagation errors through bond truncation, time-step settings, and residual checks; CT-QMC supplies an independent stochastic reference.

Method
Deterministic purification with two-site TDVP; implicit logarithmic evolution investigated separately
Software
Graft.jl, GraftImpurity.jl, GreenFunc.jl; TRIQS/CTSEG 4.0.0 reference
Bath
Nine-pole ESPRIT fit per spin
Settings
β=16, χ=96, TDVP Δτ=0.5, 65 measurement points at 0.25 spacing
Reference statistics
Eight CT-QMC replicas, 200,000 production cycles each

ImpuriTree first compresses the semicircular hybridization into a finite pole bath using ESPRIT in imaginary time. It prepares the complete interacting Gibbs state through deterministic purification, evolves that state to β/2, and measures G(τ) through operator insertions on the purification branches. Uniform-step two-site TDVP provides the production baseline, while global-Krylov bootstrapping, implicit logarithmic stepping, certified right-hand-side truncation, and residual-driven bond expansion form the low-temperature research path.

Highlight

Innovation, significance, and broader impact.

What's innovative

ImpuriTree combines a symmetry-aware tree tensor-network implementation with deterministic purification and a fail-closed ESPRIT bath fit. Its low-temperature path adds global-Krylov bond bootstrapping, implicit A-stable evolution on logarithmic grids, certified right-hand-side truncation, and residual-driven bond expansion that targets only bonds implicated by the unconverged physical residual.

Significance of output

At β=16, the purified MPS calculation tracks continuous-bath CT-QMC with RMS Green-function differences of 2.34×10⁻³–4.27×10⁻³ across weak- and strong-interaction benchmarks. The comparison isolates bath discretization as the leading visible systematic and provides a reproducible baseline for evaluating colder implicit-evolution runs.

Broader impact

A tensor-network impurity solver with explicit residual accounting can complement Monte Carlo methods where stochastic noise, low temperatures, complex orbital structure, or coupled bosonic degrees of freedom become limiting. Separating the general Graft.jl tensor-network core from GraftImpurity.jl also makes new geometries, bath representations, and evolution algorithms independently testable and reusable.
