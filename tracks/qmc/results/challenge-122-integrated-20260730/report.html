Challenge

A four-model validation ladder for criticality in open quantum matter.

Problem statement

Reproduce reliable effective-central-charge estimates across clean, quenched-disordered, and monitored critical systems, then determine whether the same validated methodology supports a universal ceff at the proposed learning-induced metal–insulator transition. The central difficulty is that the universal finite-size contribution is subleading, while disorder correlations, finite-size corrections, trajectory dependence, and anisotropy can generate precise-looking but nonuniversal fits.

Significance

Effective central charge provides a common quantitative diagnostic across otherwise different critical models. Establishing a hierarchy of known benchmarks before addressing the open transition separates failures of numerical methodology from genuinely new physics. Explicit uncertainty propagation and publication gates also prevent an inconclusive exploratory result from being overstated.

The calculation follows a validation ladder. The clean Ising model first checks normalization, thermodynamic integration, finite-size fitting, and uncertainty propagation against c=1/2. Quenched disorder is then introduced through the Nishimori random-bond Ising model, where disorder realizations must be resampled at their true independence level. The weak self-dual monitored model tests state-conditioned Born sampling, Gaussian-state invariants, and self-duality. Only after these benchmarks does the workflow analyze the learning-induced transition using independent Casimir and entanglement estimators.

The universal coefficient is extracted from subleading finite-size terms rather than from the much larger bulk contribution. Correction terms and multiple size windows are therefore retained to expose finite-size drift. Bootstrap resampling propagates correlations through the complete nonlinear fit. A result is publishable only when phase location, numerical invariants, fit stability, anisotropy calibration, and independent-estimator agreement all pass.

Highlight

What the four-model comparison contributes.

Innovation

A single validation hierarchy connects exact transfer matrices, cluster Monte Carlo, quenched random-transfer products, and Born-correlated Gaussian trajectories. Every stochastic simulation uses reproducible Xoshiro256++ streams, while Python is restricted to frozen-data fitting, bootstrap analysis, and visualization.

Significant output

Three progressively harder benchmarks reproduce their reference central charges: clean Ising c=0.498739, Nishimori RBIM ceff=0.456469, and weak self-dual ceff=0.444107. The learning-induced transition does not pass the same validation standard. Its failed phase-bracketing, anisotropy, and estimator-consistency gates identify what must improve before claiming a universal coefficient.

Broader impact

The workflow provides a reusable template for numerical studies of disordered and monitored criticality: validate on known universality classes, resample at the true independence level, expose finite-size and fitting-window dependence, cross-check universal quantities with distinct observables, and define claim gates before interpreting results. This structure is applicable to localization transitions, monitored circuits, random tensor networks, and other finite-size scaling problems.
