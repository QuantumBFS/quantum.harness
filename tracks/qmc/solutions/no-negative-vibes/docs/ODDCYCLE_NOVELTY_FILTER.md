# Minimal novelty filter for the oddcycle interval family

This audit concerns the continuum alphabet

\[
\mathcal A_I=\{B(z),B(z)^{\mathsf T}:99/100\leq z\leq101/100\}
\]

certified in `SYMMETRIC_ODDCYCLE_INTERVAL_FAMILY.md`.  It deliberately
separates exact exclusions from open classification questions.  The fixed
member \(B=B(1)\) belongs to the alphabet, so one obstruction for \(B\) is
enough to rule out a common similarity reduction of the whole alphabet.

## Result hierarchy

| Candidate explanation | Status | Exact evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed split-orthogonal group | excluded | \(\det B=8\), and the common bilinear-invariance system has nullity zero |
| Standard one-particle Kramers mechanism | excluded in the five-dimensional representation | odd dimension; the common commutant of \(B,B^{\mathsf T}\) contains only scalars |
| Simultaneous block or conjugate-pair reduction | excluded | words of length at most five span all \(M_5(\mathbb R)\) |
| Diagonal sign/positive gauge to nonnegative matrices | excluded | the directed \(2\to4\to3\to2\) cycle has invariant product \(-1\) |
| Similarity to a totally nonnegative alphabet | excluded | \(B\) has only one real eigenvalue and two nonreal conjugate pairs |
| Broad contraction-semigroup or Majorana classification | **not excluded here** | requires a separate 10-Majorana/generator-level audit |

## 1. Split-orthogonal group

If a nondegenerate bilinear form \(\eta\) obeyed

\[
B_i^{\mathsf T}\eta B_i=\eta
\]

for every letter, determinants would give \((\det B_i)^2=1\).  Instead
\(\det B(z)=8\), invariant under similarity.  At \(z=1\), exact solution of
the stronger linear system for an arbitrary, not necessarily symmetric,
\(5\times5\) form gives

\[
\{X:B^{\mathsf T}XB=X,\;BXB^{\mathsf T}=X\}=\{0\}.
\]

Thus there is no hidden common nonzero bilinear form, let alone a
nondegenerate split form.  This excludes the group mechanism itself.  It
does not by itself exclude every one-sided indefinite contraction cone.

## 2. Standard Kramers pairing

A standard antiunitary \(T=UK\) with \(T^2=-I\) requires even complex
one-particle dimension.  The present determinant acts on five modes.
There is also a basis-independent fixed-alphabet obstruction: exact linear
algebra gives

\[
\{X:XB=BX,\;XB^{\mathsf T}=B^{\mathsf T}X\}
=\mathbb R I.
\]

Since the atoms are real, the linear part \(U\) of any common antiunitary
symmetry would lie in this commutant.  A scalar \(U\) has
\(U\overline U=|u|^2I\), never \(-I\).  Hence the theorem is not standard
Kramers positivity in disguise on the stated five-dimensional space.
Artificial flavor doubling is a different model, not a similarity of this
one-particle alphabet.

As diagnostics, the exact characteristic polynomial is

\[
\lambda^5-2\lambda^4+\lambda^3-7\lambda^2+16\lambda-8.
\]

It is square-free and has one positive real root, no negative real root,
and two nonreal conjugate pairs.  The one-letter weight is
\(\det(I+B)=35\), not an integer square.  These diagnostics agree with,
but are not needed for, the commutant obstruction.

## 3. Obvious semigroup and similarity reductions

The 25 explicitly frozen words through length five span a rank-25 space of
matrices.  Therefore the real algebra generated by \(B,B^{\mathsf T}\) is
all \(M_5(\mathbb R)\).  In particular, there is no simultaneous invariant
block decomposition or fixed non-scalar commuting linear symmetry.

The negative edge also cannot be removed by a diagonal sign or positive
scaling gauge.  Under \(S^{-1}BS\), products around a directed cycle are
unchanged, while

\[
B_{4,2}B_{3,4}B_{2,3}=(-1)(1)(1)=-1.
\]

An entrywise nonnegative matrix cannot have that cycle product.  Finally,
similarity to a totally nonnegative matrix is impossible because totally
nonnegative matrices have real nonnegative spectrum, whereas \(B\) has
four nonreal eigenvalues.

These facts exclude the most immediate block, monomial-gauge, and
totally-nonnegative explanations.  They do **not** say that the alphabet
is “not a semigroup”: its products obviously form a semigroup.  The
novelty question is whether its positivity theorem reduces to a previously
known sufficient semigroup condition.

## 4. What remains open

This minimal filter does not yet exclude:

1. the full Wei-2024 contraction inequalities after mapping the five
   number-conserving modes to ten Majoranas;
2. Majorana reflection positivity after a fixed complex orthogonal
   Majorana basis change;
3. an irreducible cone-preserving semigroup equivalence not visible as a
   block, bilinear-form, or diagonal-gauge reduction;
4. equivalence at the physical-model level to a known fermion-bag,
   worldline, or loop construction.

The existence of a number-conserving-to-Majorana representation is
automatic and is not itself a Majorana sign-free mechanism.  Promotion to
“new beyond known Majorana/semigroup classes” should wait until the first
two inequalities are audited on the physical generators or until a
literature-equivalence check resolves them.
